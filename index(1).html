About

I am an Assistant Professor in the Construction Management Program within the Department of Sustainable Resources Management at SUNY-ESF, and I lead the Sustainable Engineering & Built Environment (SEBE) group, which develops lower-carbon construction materials and studies how buildings use energy, so that the built environment can cut emissions without giving up strength, durability or affordability.

I joined SUNY-ESF in Fall 2023. I earned my PhD in Civil and Environmental Engineering from The Hong Kong Polytechnic University in 2017, and held research and postdoctoral positions in Hong Kong and the United States before coming to ESF. I welcome inquiries from motivated undergraduate and graduate students (see the SEBE group section).

Education and appointments

I earned my PhD in Civil and Environmental Engineering from The Hong Kong Polytechnic University in 2017. I then served as a Postdoctoral Fellow at The University of Hong Kong (2017 to 2018), a Postdoctoral Fellow in the Department of Civil, Environmental, and Construction Engineering at the University of Central Florida (2019), and a Research Assistant Professor in the Department of Civil Engineering at The University of Hong Kong (2019 to 2022). From 2022 to 2023 I was a Research Fellow in the Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering at the University of Pittsburgh. Since 2023, I have been an Assistant Professor in the Construction Management Program, Department of Sustainable Resources Management, at SUNY-ESF.

Highlights

New article published in Cleaner Engineering and Technology

2026 · Elsevier

Excited to share that our research article, “Designing sustainable concrete to maximize the use of local waste materials: Mechanical, durability, and sustainability-based multicriteria analysis,” has been published in Cleaner Engineering and Technology (Elsevier). Read the article.

Can we significantly reduce the carbon footprint of concrete while maintaining its mechanical performance and improving durability? This was the central question behind our research.

With the growing need to decarbonize the construction industry and minimize waste disposal, we investigated the potential of utilizing locally available waste materials, ground glass powder (GGP) and ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS), as supplementary cementitious materials to replace up to 80% of Portland limestone cement (PLC).

Some key findings from our study:

  • Significant carbon reduction
  • Enhanced durability
  • Reduced deterioration potential

New article published in Smart and Sustainable Built Environment

2026 · Emerald Publishing

Our research paper, “Evaluating the potential for circular economy integration into the building LCA tools in North America,” has been published in Smart and Sustainable Built Environment by Emerald Publishing. Read the article. DOI: 10.1108/SASBE-11-2025-0723

Why is this study worth reading?

Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) is increasingly being used to quantify and reduce the environmental impacts of buildings. However, as the construction industry moves toward a circular economy (CE), an important question remains: are the LCA tools we currently use actually equipped to evaluate circular construction practices?

In this study, we examined 15 commonly adopted building and building-material LCA tools in North America, including One Click LCA, Athena, TallyLCA, EC3, GREET LCA, BEES, and others. Rather than simply comparing their existing features, we developed a new evaluation framework consisting of 17 indicators across five criteria to systematically assess their capabilities, limitations, and potential for circular economy integration.

I believe this work can be useful for researchers, LCA practitioners, architects, engineers, sustainability professionals, and policymakers who are trying to select appropriate LCA tools and understand how these tools need to evolve to support a more circular built environment.
